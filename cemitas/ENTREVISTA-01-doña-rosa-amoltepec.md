# Entrevista de campo - Doña Rosa

## 📋 Field Pain Validation Sheet: [INTERVIEW #01]

### 1. General Info
- **Community:** San Cristóbal Amoltepec, Oaxaca
- **Artisan/Farmer:** Doña Rosa (62 years old, backstrap weaver)
- **Native Language:** Tu'un Savi (Mixteco) - speaks basic Spanish

### 2. The Economic Pain & Injustice (Real Numbers)
- **Craft piece / Product:** Traditional wool rebozo dyed with natural cochineal.
- **Time invested:** 3 weeks of daily work (~90 manual hours).
- **Out-of-pocket material cost:** $450 MXN (raw wool, cochineal, alum).
- **Price paid by the "Coyote" (Middleman):** **$800 MXN**
  *(The artisan only makes $350 MXN net profit after materials and labor, equivalent to approximately $3.80 MXN/hour.)*
- **Price the middleman resells it for:** **$3,800 to $4,500 MXN** (+375% to +463% markup)
- **Fair price the artisan asks for:** **$2,200 MXN**

### 3. Specific Abuses Reported
- "The coyote takes 5 rebozos on credit and says he will pay at the end of the month, but in reality sometimes pays after 3 months."
- "If the wool has a tiny natural variation, he says it is defective and cuts the price by half."
- "Tourists in town ask for prices as low as $300 pesos for a piece that took a month to weave."
- "He buys at starvation prices and then resells with a large profit while we are left without cash for basic needs."

### 4. Cultural Plagiarism
- Factory-print copies from Puebla are reproducing traditional Amoltepec rain and mountain patterns and selling them as "handmade Mixtec crafts" in tourist markets.
- The designs are being copied without consent, and the artisan receives no recognition or compensation.

### 5. Audio Testimony in Native Language
- **Audio file:** `audio_testimonies/ENT-01-dona-rosa-amoltepec.m4a`
- **Transcription & Spanish translation:**
  *"Mi abuela me enseñó a tejer en el telar de cintura cuando tenía 10 años. Cada símbolo representa la lluvia y el maíz. No queremos que los coyotes se enriquezcan mientras nosotros no alcanzamos ni para el hilo."*

  *"My grandmother taught me to weave on the waist loom when I was 10. Each symbol represents the rain and cornfield. We do not want middlemen to become rich while we cannot even afford thread."*
